A marine protected area has 5 different marine species populations, including dolphins, turtles, sharks, corals, and seagrass. The main goal of the marine protected area is to maximize the total population of these 5 marine species through proper resource allocation. The resources of the marine protected area include personnel and funding, which are used for the conservation and research of marine species. Each marine species population requires a certain amount of resources for conservation: dolphins require 10 units of personnel and 15 units of funding, turtles require 15 units of personnel and 10 units of funding, sharks require 20 units of personnel and 20 units of funding, corals require 25 units of personnel and 30 units of funding, and seagrass requires 30 units of personnel and 25 units of funding. The marine protected area has a total of 100 units of personnel and 150 units of funding available. In addition, the marine protected area needs to consider the following constraints: - Resource allocation must meet the conservation requirements of each marine species population. - The total allocation of resources cannot exceed their available quantities. - Protection of corals and seagrass is particularly important, and their resource allocation should not be less than 50% of their requirements. Note that resource allocation units are integers and cannot be fractional. What is the optimal resource allocation strategy that can achieve the conservation goals of the marine protected area while satisfying the above constraints?
